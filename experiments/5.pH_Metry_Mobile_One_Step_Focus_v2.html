Theory: Titration of Strong Acid Vs Strong Base by pH Metry

1. Combined Glass Electrode & Calibration

A pH meter measures potential difference between a hydrogen ion-sensitive glass electrode and an Ag/AgCl reference electrode immersed in the solution according to the Nernst equation:

E = E₀ - 2.303 (RT / F) × pH

Two standard buffer solutions are used for two-point calibration:

  • Buffer pH 9.20: Adjusts zero-intercept asymmetry using the CAL knob.
  • Buffer pH 4.00: Evaluates response slope. Any difference $\Delta = |4.00 - X|$ is compensated equally: half with the % SLOPE knob and the remaining half with the CAL knob.

2. Titration Principle & Differential Endpoint Detection

When strong hydrochloric acid (HCl) is titrated against sodium hydroxide (NaOH):

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
(H⁺ + Cl⁻) + (Na⁺ + OH⁻) → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ + H₂O

Near the equivalence point, the pH changes sharply over minute volume additions. By calculating the first derivative:

Differential Quotient = ΔpH / ΔV

Plotting $\Delta\text{pH}/\Delta V$ against the volume of NaOH yields a sharp maximum peak at the equivalence point ($V_e$), giving high endpoint accuracy.

Quantitative Formulae

Normality of HCl: N(HCl) = [N(NaOH) × V_e] / V(HCl)
Strength of HCl (g/L): Strength = N(HCl) × 36.5 g/mol
