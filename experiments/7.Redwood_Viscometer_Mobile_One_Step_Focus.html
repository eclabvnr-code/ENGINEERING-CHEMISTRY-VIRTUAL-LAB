Theory: Determination of Viscosity Using Redwood Viscometer No. 1

1. Principle of the Redwood Viscometer

Viscosity is determined by measuring the efflux time ($t$, in seconds) taken for exactly 50 mL of oil to discharge drop-by-drop through a standard agate jet under specified head and temperature into a 50 mL Kohlrausch flask.

2. Governing Equations & Viscometer Constants

The kinematic viscosity ($\nu$) in centistokes ($\text{cSt}$ or $\text{mm}^2/\text{s}$) is computed using empirical formulas:

For efflux time $t \le 100\text{ seconds}$:    $\nu = 0.264 \times t - \dfrac{190}{t}$
For efflux time $t > 100\text{ seconds}$:    $\nu = 0.247 \times t - \dfrac{50}{t}$

3. Density & Absolute (Dynamic) Viscosity

From the mass of 50 mL collected oil ($m = W_2 - W_1$), density ($\rho$) and dynamic viscosity ($\eta$) are calculated:

$\rho = \dfrac{m}{50.0}\text{ g/cm}^3$
$\text{Dynamic Viscosity } \eta = \nu \times \rho\text{ centipoise (cP)}$
