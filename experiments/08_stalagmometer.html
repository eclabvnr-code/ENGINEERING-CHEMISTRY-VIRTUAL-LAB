Theory: Determination of Surface Tension by Stalagmometer (Drop-Weight & Drop-Number Methods)

1. Principle of the Stalagmometer

Surface tension (γ) is the force per unit length acting perpendicularly across any line on the liquid surface. In a stalagmometer with a ground-flat circular dropping tip, a drop slowly forms, necks down, and detaches when its downward gravitational weight overcomes the upward perimeter surface tension force (Tate's Law):

W = m · g = 2 · π · r · γ · f
where m is single drop mass, r is the outer radius of the ground disc tip, γ is surface tension, and f is the Harkins-Brown correction factor.

2. Direct Drop-Weight Method (Collected into Pycnometer)

By collecting the total mass of drops discharged between calibration Mark A and Mark B directly into a pre-weighed specific gravity bottle:

Weight of empty bottle = w₁ g
Weight of bottle + water drops (A → B) = w₂ g ⟹ Mass of water W_W = (w₂ - w₁)
Weight of bottle + liquid drops (A → B) = w₃ g ⟹ Mass of liquid W_L = (w₃ - w₁)

γ_L / γ_W = (W_L / W_W) × (n_W / n_L) = [(w₃ - w₁) / (w₂ - w₁)] × (n_W / n_L)

Because the fixed volume of Bulb B between Mark A and Mark B is identical for both liquids, the mass ratio also gives the exact density ratio d_L / d_W:

γ_L = γ_W × (n_W / n_L) × [(w₃ - w₁) / (w₂ - w₁)]

Standard Surface Tension of Distilled Water (γ_W at 25°C) = 72.00 dynes/cm (or mN/m).
