Principle & Reaction Mechanism: Preparation of Phenol-Formaldehyde Resin (Bakelite)

Principle

Phenolic resins (phenoplasts) are condensation polymerization products of phenol with formaldehyde. Polymers that change irreversibly on heating into hard, rigid, and infusible 3-dimensional cross-linked networks are called thermosetting polymers.

Three-Stage Formation of Bakelite

Step 1 — Formation of Methylol Phenol Derivatives:
C₆H₅OH + HCHO → o-HO·C₆H₄·CH₂OH + p-HO·C₆H₄·CH₂OH

Step 2 — Formation of Linear Novolac Resin (Acid-Catalyzed):
n (HO·C₆H₄·CH₂OH) → —[C₆H₃(OH)—CH₂]ₙ— + (n − 1) H₂O

Step 3 — Cross-Linking to 3D Thermosetting Bakelite:
Further condensation forms extensive —CH₂— methylene bridges between phenolic rings, yielding a hard, pink, insoluble 3D network polymer.
